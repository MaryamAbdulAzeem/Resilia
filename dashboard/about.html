The device

A safety helmet that feels the heat before you do.

1 · Solar strip

Thin panel on the shell keeps it charged all shift.

2 · Forehead sensor pad

Soft pad in the sweatband reads heart rate and skin temperature.

3 · Motion + vibration

Tracks workload and buzzes alert patterns you feel, not read.

4 · Brim LED + button

A glow at the edge of vision; one press to acknowledge, hold for help.

The brim light shows the crew's most urgent state, live.

Three states. No phone, no reading.

Each state has its own light and its own feel, so it works in any language, under any noise. Live counts come from the crew on shift now.

How it connects

No WiFi on site. No phone per worker.

Helmets talk to one nearby gateway; the gateway uses the mobile network to reach Resilia's AI.

On the worker

Resilia helmet

Heart rate, forehead temperature and movement, sampled every few seconds.

 
Bluetooth LE tiny power use
On the site

Site gateway

A small solar receiver per work zone, or the supervisor's phone running Resilia.

 
5G / LTE mobile network
In the cloud

Resilia AI

Adds live site weather (WBGT), predicts risk ~15 min ahead and decides what to do.

Big site? One gateway per work zone covers Bluetooth's range.

Signal lost? The helmet keeps readings, alerts on its own, and syncs later.

What's real in this prototype

The helmet above is the concept. For the hackathon, software stands in for the hardware.

Simulated

Helmets & gateways

A simulator plays 15 workers: heart rate, forehead temperature, movement, solar battery and occasional SOS presses, sent every 5 seconds through four simulated site gateways.

Working code

Risk engine & AI forecast

Scores every worker from their readings and WBGT, and a machine-learning model (random forest) predicts heat stress up to 15 minutes ahead.

Working code

AI agent & dashboards

The agent acts within set rules and asks before big steps; its breaks, water runs and crew pauses really change the simulated workers.
